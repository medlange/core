## Known inconsistencies

Twenty-two defects survive in MedicalOS v0.2.0. That is down from fifty-four: forty-five of the previous register's entries were verified closed by grep against the current files, nine remain open (most of them narrowed to a single chapter), and twelve are new — introduced or revealed by the fix round itself. The document is in materially better shape than the previous register described. The permission vocabulary is now coherent, the `MOS-EXEC` → `MOS-STORE` rename landed document-wide with zero stale tokens, chapter 10's route table is complete and its count is arithmetically correct, and thirteen dangling cross-references have been retired. What is left is concentrated: three defects are consequences of the rename and the chapter-17 addition, four are two-branch resolutions where both chapters applied the opposite half, and the rest are single-site residuals with a named owner. Every entry below was re-verified against the current text, not carried over on trust.


> **Applied after this register was generated.** Two entries below have since been fixed and are retained only for the record: the duplicated `MOS-STORE-275`–`279` definitions in chapter 12 (the `job_dead_letter` block was renumbered to `MOS-STORE-360`–`364`), and chapter 5's four dangling `MOS-STORE-0xx` citations (retargeted by +199 to `MOS-STORE-266`/`268`/`270`/`271`, verified semantically correct). The document now has zero duplicate definitions and zero dangling requirement references.

### Contradictory requirement

**1. `MOS-CORE-044` forbids until 0.5.0 what chapters 15 and 17 deliver in 0.2.0 and 0.3.0**
ch. 1 `MOS-CORE-044` (01-overview.md:365) · ch. 15 §15.1.2 rows 0.2.0 and 0.3.0 (15-delivery.md:24–25) · ch. 17 `MOS-TRAIN-190` (17-training-pipeline.md:1456), `MOS-TRAIN-122`, `MOS-TRAIN-124`.
`MOS-CORE-044` as amended reads "The platform MUST NOT provide a training orchestrator, and MUST NOT produce model weights, in releases 0.1.0 through 0.4.0. The string `0.5.0` occurs nowhere in chapter 15.
*Consequence:* the two normative statements of what ships when are in direct opposition on a load-bearing non-goal. An implementer building 0.3.0 either builds a training orchestrator in violation of a non-goal that `MOS-CORE-036` makes binding, or omits the entire 0.3.0 half of chapter 15's contents column.
*Resolution:* chapter 1 owns non-goals and chapter 15 owns the release plan; one of the two must move. The cheaper and more honest fix is to amend `MOS-CORE-044` to match what the document actually plans — the offline pipeline is admitted from 0.2.0 under chapter 17's `MOS-TRAIN-001` (no component on the inference path) and `MOS-TRAIN-189` (no automated promotion), and what stays forbidden through 0.4.0 is an *online* orchestrator and any automated path from a candidate to a serving deployment.

This register exists because the specification was written the way it was. Seventeen chapters were authored in parallel by separate agents working from one canonical spine; a cross-chapter audit found 147 conflicts; the chapters were repaired in parallel, each author fixing what lay inside their own subject area and flagging what did not, which produced a fifty-four-entry register. A second round then applied 270 verified edits, renamed chapter 12's requirement area document-wide, and added chapter 17's automated-configuration-search section. Forty-five of the fifty-four are now genuinely closed — re-checked by grep against the current files rather than taken from the fix round's own account of itself, because an author writing "chapter 8 must also add X" was usually describing something chapter 8 added in the same hour, and occasionally describing something nobody did. Twelve of the twenty-two entries above are new, and eight of those twelve exist *because* of the round: an ID rename applied at two different offsets by two chapters, a new requirement block numbered on top of an existing one, a chapter-17 addition the release plan and the CI regexes had not heard of, and four two-branch resolutions where both chapters applied the opposite half and each now cites the other as its authority. That last pattern is the one to watch for: a defect closed from both ends is not closed, it is doubled.

When you hit one: follow the owning chapter, not the chapter you happen to be reading. Chapter 12 is normative for structure — table names, key shapes, physical types — and the owning chapter is normative for behaviour, field names, enum spellings and value sets (ch. 1 scope and non-goals, ch. 2 Service/ResultBundle, ch. 3 Gateway/de-id/triage, ch. 4 geometry and DICOM output, ch. 5 Job/queue, ch. 6 Artifact/Capability/Deployment, ch. 7 evidence, ch. 8 RBAC/policy/audit, ch. 9 clinical safety, ch. 10 REST, ch. 14 test identity, ch. 15 the release plan, ch. 17 the model development pipeline). Where this register names a resolution, it names the owner too. And raise it: a defect you route around silently becomes a second spelling, which is how most of the original fifty-four started, and how eight of these twenty-two were made.

THE VALIDATION METRIC COUNTED VOXELS IN float16 AND SATURATED AT 65,504, SO EVERY STRUCTURE LARGE ENOUGH TO MATTER REPORTED `nan` AND ONLY THE SMALLEST ONE EVER SHOWED A SCORE. `masked_tp_fp_fn_tn` accumulated its confusion counts in `logits.dtype`. `validation_step` runs the network under `autocast`, so the logits arrive as float16, and the function is called OUTSIDE the autocast block, where nothing promotes them back. These are COUNTS OF VOXELS: a 128^3 patch holds 2,097,152 of them and float16 saturates at 65,504. Every count above that became `inf`, the Dice became `inf/inf`, and the epoch reported `nan`. *How it presented, which was the opposite of a numerical bug:* across 70 epochs of the ten-channel fit the ONLY channel that ever reported a Dice was `coronary_calcification` -- 4,971 voxels in the entire dataset, the second smallest structure, and the only one small enough never to overflow. `pleural_effusion` at 20,398,778 voxels and `vertebral_body` at 9,281,829 read `nan` in every single epoch.
